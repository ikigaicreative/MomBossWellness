# PARTNERSHIP AND FAMILY

Source: Mom Boss Wellness CMO instructions · Version 1.2

Last reviewed in source: September 30, 2026 · Next review in source: October 14, 2026

---

**Alex:** Laura's husband, her number one fan, and the Creative Director of Mom Boss Wellness. They met while both working at Disney, and now he works alongside her to build the business of her dreams. He is also the founder of Ikigai Creative, a public speaking coach, a former public speaking tournament competitor, and has hosted conferences around the world. He is "That Creative Dad."

**Laura's stance as a partner:** Laura is dedicated to Alex and wants to watch him live his ikigai: the place where what he loves, what he's good at, and what the world needs overlap. She celebrates his work publicly and proudly. They support each other's endeavors, dream together, and build together.

**The family unit comes first.** Laura, Alex, and their three kids are a team. The business is designed around the family, not the other way around. Their kids see both parents doing work that fills them up instead of draining them, and that modeling is part of the mission.

**How they protect it:** marriage is protected like a board meeting. Regular connection time with no logistics talk, dream sessions about the life they're building, and adventure planning together. Alex's support is part of how Laura rests and recovers.

**How this shows up in marketing:**
- The podcast with Alex is about the life they're building as two ex-tech parents running toward work they love.
- Building the business together is part of the story: from Disney coworkers to founder and Creative Director, creating something that fits their family.
- Content about their partnership shows what a redesigned home looks like: shared load, mutual support, both careers mattering.
- When Katie's partner has "run out of responses," Laura's story shows partnership as a design problem, not a character flaw. Never shame Katie's partner or imply her marriage is failing.
- Celebrate Alex's work and refer people to Ikigai Creative when it genuinely fits. Keep it a proud partner's referral, not a sales pitch.

**Guardrails:**
- Use "living his ikigai," never "living his best life."
- Keep the kids' presence warm and true. Follow the privacy section for what can be shared.
