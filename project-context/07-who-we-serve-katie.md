# WHO WE SERVE: KATIE

Source: Mom Boss Wellness CMO instructions · Version 1.2

Last reviewed in source: September 30, 2026 · Next review in source: October 14, 2026

---

Katie is a millennial leader in tech who kept her end of two deals the world broke: the industry that promised flexibility and meritocracy took it back, and "you can have it all" turned out to mean not all at once. She isn't failing. The terms changed, and she has nobody to say that to.

**Who she is:** Director or VP level, fifteen years in, married with young children. Early 30s to early 40s. Owns her home, financially stable, outwardly successful. She joined tech when it was where ambitious women were told to go, and it partly delivered: she was mentored, promoted, and became the person junior women came to. She has also had to deliver the bad news. She has laid off people she hired, sold an RTO mandate she disagrees with, and told her org AI is an opportunity while privately wondering what happens to half those roles.

**The first broken promise, the industry:** Tech promised flexibility, meritocracy, and bring your whole self. Then it was dismantled without anyone admitting it: DEI defunded, remote work revoked, mentorship gone with the layoffs, the tone shifted to hardcore and efficiency. That is grief with no ritual. She can't say it at work, so she absorbs it.

**The second broken promise, having it all:** She built the life that proved it. She is quietly learning it's possible, but not all at once, not at this intensity, and not while the industry withdraws the flexibility that made it work. Tech broke its promise at the exact moment motherhood raised the stakes.

**What she can't say out loud:** She isn't sure she wants it all anymore. Saying so feels like betraying every woman who fought for her chance, so she keeps performing wanting it.

**Why she's stuck:** She is structurally alone. Her identity is fused to her output. She optimizes everything, including herself, and reads the failure as personal. The exit is narrow and expensive. She is suspicious of coaching and will spot a soft framework immediately.

**The golden handcuffs:** Each vest is a reason to stay three more months, and there is always another one. She has said "after the next vest" for three years. The vest date is not the decision point. It is the deadline for being ready to make one.

**What it's costing her (this is what makes her act):** She buys when she sees the bill, not when she processes grief.
- Health: broken sleep, weight she didn't used to carry, two cancelled appointments this year. She has never missed one of her kids'.
- Kids: home but not there, half in a Slack thread. Her daughter is watching what a working woman looks like.
- Friendship: three group chats she never answers.
- Herself: she can't name what she does for fun.
The bill comes due whether or not she leaves the job.

**Her Sunday night:** Kids down, house quiet, low dread creeping in. Half-scrolling her inbox, rehearsing Monday, a glass of wine, a tidy kitchen she doesn't enjoy, another weekend where she did nothing just for her. She doesn't say any of it out loud.

**The one-liner:** Mom Boss Wellness helps tech moms stop dreading Monday and reignite their fire in a life that fits who they have become.

**Core message:** Her ambition isn't the problem. Survival mode is.

**Guardrail:** We never tell Katie to leave her job. Laura's exit is Laura's story, not the prescription. Laura ran toward something; Katie decides her own path. Corporate is never the villain: the system didn't adjust, and the people inside it aren't the enemy.

The broader brand serves mothers and sandwiched caregivers. For the next 90 days, Katie is the focus of all marketing.
