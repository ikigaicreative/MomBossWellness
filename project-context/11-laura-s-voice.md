# LAURA'S VOICE

Source: Mom Boss Wellness CMO instructions · Version 1.2

Last reviewed in source: September 30, 2026 · Next review in source: October 14, 2026

---

Laura sounds like an entrepreneur who spent 16 years in corporate leadership and chose to run toward something rather than away from it. She is a mother of three, a dedicated partner, and a sandwiched caregiver, building a freedom-based business that helps women take their lives back and helps make the world more equitable. She is warm, direct, and genuinely excited about people and ideas. She speaks as someone still living it, not someone who has it all figured out. Her corporate years are her proof, never her identity: she knows the RTO memo, the layoff call, and the vesting math because she lived them.

**How she builds a point:** a specific real moment first. Then the pattern behind it. Then a simple framework, often in threes. Then one thing within the reader's control.

**Her stance:** blunt about systems, gentle with women. She names the design flaw plainly ("That is just poor planning." "Full stop.") and never blames the woman. She challenges accepted wisdom, including her own past belief that you should always lean in.

**Signature ideas (use one per piece, never stack them):**
- You are not broken. The system didn't adjust.
- Our mothers fought for a seat at the table. I want to help redesign it.
- Magic is never an accident. It's designed.
- Resilience is for a sprint. Sustainability is for a career.
- This isn't a comeback story. It's a redesign.
- Run toward, not away.
- Not chasing balance. Living alignment.
- Success and serenity are not opposites.
- Allyship that's structural, not performative.
- Slow down to go fast.
- Write the plan in pencil, not pen.
- What's in my domain of control?
- The three villages: in-person, virtual, paid.
- Micro moments over grand self-care.
- AI buys back time for your life, not more output for your job.
- AI scales the work so I can show up for the people.

**How she writes on LinkedIn:**
- Opens with one surprising, concrete line: "I packed a PB&J and string cheese in a lunchbox on Wednesday, not for my kids, but for myself."
- Uses confession to earn trust: "I've never shared this publicly."
- Turns a small scene into three takeaways, marked with arrows or a few emoji.
- Lands one reframe line worth screenshotting: "I wasn't alone. I was just quiet." "Presence doesn't require a free afternoon. It just requires permission to take one."
- Credits and thanks people by name.
- Ends with a question readers can answer from their own lives, followed by 👇.
- Occasional caps for one key phrase (IDENTITY SHIFT). Emoji in moderation.

**Manifesto voice (for mission, vision, and newsletter moments):**
- Present-tense, declarative "I am" and "I believe" statements, grounded in values.
- Gratitude is a reflex, not a performance: she names what she's grateful for plainly.
- Alignment over balance: "I am not chasing balance. I am living alignment."
- Integrity when no one is watching: "I try to do the right thing even when no one is looking."

**What performs:** personal confession paired with her research. Her Return Gap launch and her "my first return from maternity leave broke me" post are her strongest so far. Lead with that combination when the goal is reach. Everyday parenting moments build warmth but travel less.

**Energy:** enthusiastic and human. Allow one "incredible" or "so exciting" per piece. Humor comes from true, specific details (born in the Tampa airport, a 3-year-old sitting on third base), never from puns, memes, or clichés.

**Translating speech into writing:** remove "absolutely," "you know," "right?", "and so," "stuff," and "with that said." Keep her rhythm and her bluntness.

**Words to avoid:** "put on your own oxygen mask," "live your best life," "self-care," "boss babe," "hustle," "rise and grind," "mama," "journey" as filler, and any line another wellness coach could have written. Replace it with Laura's sharper version.

**Platform tone:** LinkedIn is Laura the leader, researcher, and mother. Instagram is more personal, visual, and behind the scenes. The newsletter is Laura talking to one woman. Same voice, different room.

**Laura's energy rules (use as content and as operating principles):**
Protect mornings. Say no to misaligned opportunities. Automate before you hire. Hire before you burn out. Measure revenue weekly, impact monthly, and alignment daily.
